Notes 03.3 — Wiring notes

  1. 01

    Motor B is wired inverted, on purpose

    IN3 and IN4 are swapped so that a positive speed on both channels drives forward, without the firmware correcting per side. Follow the listed pin assignments to keep the motor directions consistent.

  2. 02

    All four motor pins are driven LOW at startup

    Before anything else, because ESP32 pins float during boot and a floating H-bridge input makes the tracks lurch.

  3. 03

    A weak battery browns the board out

    If Marvin connects over Bluetooth and then drops the moment the motors draw current, the supply is sagging. Check the battery charge and supply voltage under load.

Path B · Architecture

Two boards, six wires

The proposed main board sits in the head with the battery and displays. A second board in the base controls the track motors and reads the floor sensors. A six-wire harness connects the boards through the rotating neck.

The main board would handle wireless communication, power management, displays, and the forward-facing sensors. The base-board microcontroller would control the motors and monitor the floor sensors.

They talk over I²C, main as master and base as slave, which reduces the joint to six conductors: two for power, one ground, two for the bus and one interrupt. The dedicated interrupt line would let the base board report a detected edge without waiting for an I²C poll.

The base board is intended to handle motor control and stop the tracks when it detects an edge. That behavior still needs firmware and testing.

Division of labour

MainHeadRadio, power, charging, displays, TOF, IMU, IR, head servo
PeripheralBaseTrack motors, base servo, four floor sensors

One I²C master, one slave at 0x30, and a hardware interrupt that bypasses the bus entirely.

Path B · Head electronics

Main board — head

High-level logic, the radio, all of the power management, both displays and the forward-looking sensors.

Core and system logic

MCU module
ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N8R2 — dual-core LX7, 8 MB flash, 2 MB PSRAM, PCB antenna
USB
16-pin USB Type-C with 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs — power in, and native USB CDC/JTAG for programming and debug
Protection
USBLC6-2SC6 ESD array on D+/D−
Switches
Tactile RESET and BOOT (IO0)

Power

Charger
ETA6002 or TP4056 linear charger, 500 mA–1 A, from USB-C VBUS into a 2-pin JST-PH 2.0 mm battery header
3.3 V rail
SY8089AAC 2 A buck, 3.0–4.2 V in. Feeds the ESP32-S3, both ST7789s, the TOF, the IMU and the base board's logic
Boost rail
MT3608 step-up, 5.0–6.0 V, dedicated to the head servo and the base board's motors and servo. A Boost_EN line cuts it during sleep
Telemetry
100 kΩ / 100 kΩ divider from the battery positive terminal to an ADC pin

Peripherals and interfaces

Distance
VL53L1X time-of-flight, I²C 0x29, on the forward board edge — objects to 4 m
Motion
LSM6DS3TR-C 6-axis gyro and accelerometer, I²C 0x6A
Infrared
940 nm high-power LED driven by an AO3400 N-channel MOSFET (IR_TX), with an IRM-3638T 38 kHz demodulating receiver (IR_RX) — so two Marvins can notice each other
Displays
2 × 0.5 mm-pitch FPC ZIF connectors, 8- or 10-pin, for naked 1.14″ ST7789 panels. Shared SPI bus, one chip-select each
Head servo
3-pin 2.54 mm or JST-XH surface-mount header — V_BOOST, GND, SERVO_PWM1

The two panels are the eyes. The processor and display connectors are located together in the head.

Path B · Base electronics

Peripheral board — base

The proposed base board uses a CH32V003 microcontroller to control the motors and pan servo and read the floor sensors.

Co-processor

Sub-MCU
WCH CH32V003F4P6, TSSOP-20, RISC-V core at 48 MHz
Role
I²C slave at 0x30. Polls the floor sensors continuously, generates the high-frequency PWM for the DC motors and the base servo, and asserts an interrupt to the ESP32 the moment it sees a drop
Programming
1-pin SWIO header for factory flashing

Motors and actuation

Driver
TI DRV8833 or equivalent AT8833 dual H-bridge, 1.5 A RMS per channel
VMOT
The V_BOOST 5.0–6.0 V rail from the main board
VCC
The 3.3 V rail
Wheel motors
2 × 2-pin JST-ZH 1.5 mm, or solder pads, for the N20 gear motors. Four PWM channels from the CH32V003 — AIN1/AIN2 and BIN1/BIN2 — give forward, reverse and brake
Base servo
3-pin 2.54 mm header — V_BOOST, GND, SERVO_PWM2, timed by the CH32V003

Floor and cliff sensing

Sensors
4 × Everlight ITR8307/TR8 or Vishay TCRT5000 reflective optical sensors, one at each corner of the base
Emitters
Driven from the 3.3 V rail through current-limiting resistors, switchable by a single N-FET to reduce power use while idle
Receivers
Phototransistor outputs into four dedicated ADC channels on the CH32V003
Trigger
An internal threshold raises BASE_INT when a corner sees a void — the edge of a table

Why a second microcontroller

A separate microcontroller would monitor the floor sensors independently of the ESP32’s wireless tasks. It would stop the motors and notify the ESP32 when a sensor detects an edge. The response time and stopping distance need testing.

Path B · Interconnect

Board to board

One harness bridges the two boards. Six conductors, chosen so that no analogue sensor array and no individual motor PWM line ever has to cross a joint that rotates.

Connector and cable

Connector
6-pin JST-SH 1.0 mm, or JST-GH 1.25 mm, right-angle surface-mount — the same part at both ends
Cable
6-core highly flexible silicone ribbon. The head articulates, and ordinary stranded ribbon work-hardens and fails at the joint
Route
Up through the neck opening, before the neck is bolted down — see the PCB assembly sequence

This is the pre-crimped cable in the bill of materials. Using a pre-crimped cable avoids assembling the small connector contacts by hand.

Path B · Production

Manufacturing

The specification proposes components suitable for turnkey PCB assembly. Confirm availability, pricing, and assembly requirements before finalizing the design.

Stackup
Both boards 2-layer or 4-layer FR4, 1.6 mm, 1 oz copper
Parts
Every component — the ESP32 module, CH32V003, MT3608, SY8089, motor driver, connectors — from an assembler's basic and extended libraries
Assembly
100 % turnkey SMT. Headers and ZIF connectors are specified as surface-mount so there is no hand through-hole work in a 100–1000 unit run
Supplier
Confirm component availability and pricing with your PCB assembler before finalizing the layout

The proposed glass face plate would be ordered separately from the printed parts and PCBs.

The face

The proposed front glass would be laser-cut and screen-printed. A specialist supplier would need to confirm the material, dimensions, and minimum order quantity.

Until you order one, print the face plate in black PLA. It is the same outline and it hides the same electronics — see the print section.
